## Abstract

To enhance predictive accuracy and credibility of ship fuel consumption, this study proposes a framework that integrates multisource heterogeneous data, Simulated Annealing and Monte Carlo Feature Selection (SAMCFS), and multidimensional interpretability analysis. Firstly, inverse distance weighting is used to fuse ship noon reports, Automatic Identification System (AIS) records, ECMWF Reanalysis v5 (ERA5), and Copernicus Marine Service products, thereby constructing a comprehensive dataset of actual voyages. Secondly, the SAMCFS algorithm is developed to overcome the limitations of conventional methods that often become trapped in local optimum in high-dimensional contexts. Finally, for energy management and optimisation, a multidimensional interpretability analysis method is developed that combines feature importance assessment, shapley additive explanations, and partial dependence plots to elucidate the decision-making process and feature interactions within the optimal light gradient boosting machine prediction model. The proposed framework delivers accurate and interpretable prediction results, supporting improvements in ship energy efficiency and the adoption of low carbon technologies. Furthermore, it provides policymakers with quantitative evidence for decision making, aiding in the formulation of carbon control measures aligned with the “2023 IMO Strategy on Reducing GHG Emissions from Ships”, while promoting the development of green shipping corridors and the sustainable transition of the global maritime sector.
